## Correctness: supported

The mathematical rule is a total deterministic Karp reduction from Q1 to Q2, with recovery from every valid target witness. No blocking proof or implementation defect was found.

In `work/proof.md:7–31`, the exceptional set is defined relative to the supplied order, not to presumed consecutive copies. Removing the first and last original vertices ensures strict inequalities l < u and v < r for a putative residual nested pair uv, xy. If v < b or a < u, a mandatory apex edge surrounds both edges. Otherwise u < a < b < v. The six weakly increasing region pairs for x,y among left-of-a, between-apices, and right-of-b are exactly the six listed cases. Each creates strict nesting among three distinct edges. In cases 5 and 6, the deleted boundary vertex exists because x < a or b < y, respectively; avoiding that vertex gives the strict inequality needed for six distinct endpoints. No claim about queue colors beyond the impossibility of a three-rainbow is required. This establishes the deletion lemma for all finite simple X, independently of the bounded checks.

The forward witness in `work/proof.md:35–38` is valid: within a copy its original edges do not nest, different consecutive copies have disjoint order intervals, and apex edges either share an apex, cross, or are disjoint. The apex-to-apex edge shares an endpoint with every other apex edge. Thus the designated apex queue has no nesting.

For the converse (`work/proof.md:40–44`), at most four of five disjoint copies meet S. Restricting the supplied order to any untouched copy eliminates all original nesting by the lemma. Relabeling that copy gives a one-queue order of G, regardless of the supplied edge coloring or interleaving. This proves the NO implication by contraposition and proves universal witness recovery, rather than merely existence of some conveniently arranged target witness.

`work/algorithm.py` implements those maps directly. Matrix validation rejects non-square, nonsymmetric, nonbinary, noninteger, and looped matrices. In particular, booleans are rejected as matrix entries. Malformed JSON and decoding failures map to the fixed malformed target, as do malformed parsed source objects. For legal sources, the matrix is exactly five disjoint copies joined to two adjacent apices. The empty source maps to K2 and extracts an empty witness. The extractor assumes a valid target witness, as the contract explicitly permits; omitting target-witness validation does not weaken that promise. It locates both apices, identifies exactly the stated exceptional original vertices, selects an untouched copy deterministically, and emits a zero for every lexicographically enumerated source edge. It uses neither a source solver nor external state.

The graph has 5n+2 vertices and 5m+10n+1 edges. Matrix encoding and construction use O(n²) entries/word operations, with constant-size empty-instance handling. Selecting the copy and its order uses O(n) word operations. The complete command additionally validates/reads the source matrix and emits m queue labels. The conservative O(n² log(n+2)) bit bound in `work/proof.md:62–69`, plus reading the actual input bytes, is polynomial and accommodates these costs for legal inputs. Malformed inputs are scanned/rejected in polynomial encoded length. Target witnesses require O(n log(n+2)+m) bits. The target decision language is in NP by direct permutation and same-queue-pair verification. There is no exponential source search hidden in either map.

## Novelty: supported within documented coverage

The contribution eligible to advance is the exceptional-vertex lemma plus replication and witness recovery. The two-apex idea and the NP-hardness conclusion had already been proposed in a thesis; neither should be presented as having no antecedent. The checked counterexample invalidates that particular single-copy equivalence, so this is more than implementing its asserted reduction.

Primary sources inspected, with search/retrieval date 2026-09-15:

1. Heath and Rosenberg, *Laying Out Graphs Using Queues* (1992), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1137/0221055), [primary paper copy](https://courses.cs.vt.edu/cs5974/Fall2022/Resources/HeathPapers/HeathRosenberg_SIAMJComput_1992.pdf). Theorem 2.3 gives the rainbow characterization for a fixed order; Theorem 3.2 characterizes Q1 as arched leveled-planarity; Corollary 3.9 establishes Q1 recognition NP-completeness. Those statements provide the baseline and an equivalent source formulation, not the present threshold-raising map. Retrieved PDF and text are `baseline.pdf` and `baseline.txt` here.
2. Philipp de Col, *Algorithms and Drawings for Mixed Linear Layouts of Graphs* (2019), [university repository](https://repositum.tuwien.at/handle/20.500.12708/13807), Theorem 3, printed pp. 19–20. I retrieved and read the exact proof (`thesis.pdf`, `thesis.txt`). The construction is the single-copy join. Its p. 20 inference that both apices outside the chosen rainbow force their connecting edge to nest its outer edge omits placement on the same side. The independently verified six-vertex counterexample refutes the proposed decision equivalence, regardless of other local repairs. It does not refute the NP-hardness conclusion.
3. De Col, Klute, and Nöllenburg, [*Mixed Linear Layouts: Complexity, Heuristics, and Experiments*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.08938), Section 2, Theorems 1–2, pp. 3–4. The first treats two stacks plus one queue; the second preserves hardness under adding pages when the vertex order is fixed. Neither establishes the pure Q2 free-order result. Its stated contributions and theorem statements omit the thesis's pure-two-queue claim.
4. Depian, Fink, Ganian, and Surianarayanan, [*Linear Layouts Revisited: Stacks, Queues, and Exact Algorithms*](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol351-esa2025/LIPIcs.ESA.2025.15/LIPIcs.ESA.2025.15.pdf), ESA 2025, Theorems 1–3, pp. 15:2–15:4. These are structural-parameter, width-parameter, and exact one-queue algorithms. The repeated-component/Ramsey pruning discussion does concern multiple copies, but its bounded vertex-integrity premise and layout-extension objective do not imply this five-copy threshold reduction. Retrieved as `esa2025.pdf` and `esa2025.txt`.
5. Alam et al., [*The Mixed Page Number of Graphs*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.04993), Section 3, Theorem 3, pp. 3–4. Its hardness reduction uses matchings and a prescribed order, with the number of mixed pages as part of the input. Allowing stack pages and fixing the order are substantive differences. A free-order matching itself always has a one-queue layout by putting each edge's endpoints consecutively, so matching hardness here cannot directly establish Q2 recognition hardness.
6. Bekos et al., [*On the Recognition of Outerplanar Graphs with Queue Number 1*](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.24297v1), 27 July 2026, Theorem 1 and Corollary 2 in the introduction and Section 3. This additional recent primary source establishes Q1 hardness even for outerplanar graphs of pathwidth at most five. It strengthens the source baseline and settles related parameterized questions; it does not establish hardness at threshold two. In fact, its introduction emphasizes that all outerplanar graphs admit two queues, explaining why inclusion alone is inadequate.
